System Architecture & Methodology Flow

End-to-End Machine Learning Pipeline from Ingestion to Prediction

1
Authentic Domestic Ingestion:

Removed obsolete 2013 US data (Newark, JFK, LGA). Replaced with authentic domestic flights focused on Tamil Nadu (CJB, MAA, IXM, TRZ) and key connecting metros.

2
DGCA 15-Minute Threshold Standard:

Labeled flights as delayed (1) only if arrival delay exceeds 15 minutes, conforming strictly to DGCA and FAA international standards.

3
Class Imbalance Compensation:

Configured class_weight='balanced_subsample' in Random Forest and scale_pos_weight in XGBoost to handle the 77:23 on-time/delayed distribution.

4
Dual-Head Inference & Explainability:

Classification model predicts delay probability; regression model predicts delay duration in minutes; rule-based explainability decomposes contributory factors for travelers.

Viva Defense Reference

Guide & External Examiner Viva-Voce Cheat Sheet

Prepared structured answers for academic evaluation committees and professors.

1 Why did you replace the original dataset with Tamil Nadu & Indian domestic flight data?

Answer: The original repository contained 2013 flights from New York (Newark, JFK) which are completely irrelevant to Indian aviation and difficult to explain to local university guides. We customized the dataset to reflect genuine Tamil Nadu routes (Coimbatore CJB, Chennai MAA, Madurai IXM, Trichy TRZ) and major domestic airlines (IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet, Akasa Air) along with authentic meteorological factors like the Tamil Nadu North-East Monsoon.

2 What is the definition of a "delayed" flight in this project?

Answer: According to DGCA (Directorate General of Civil Aviation, India) and international FAA/ICAO benchmarks, a flight is officially categorized as "delayed" only if its arrival time exceeds the scheduled arrival time by more than 15 minutes. Any arrival within 15 minutes is treated as on-time.

3 Why do tree ensembles (Random Forest / XGBoost) outperform Logistic Regression?

Answer: Flight delay causality involves strong non-linear interactions. For example, rain in Chennai during off-peak hours may not cause delay, but rain combined with the 18:00 evening peak hour and tight aircraft turnaround triggers heavy delay. Decision trees capture non-linear thresholds and feature cross-products naturally without imposing linearity assumptions.

4 How did you address the class imbalance problem?

Answer: In domestic aviation, ~77% of flights are on-time and ~23% are delayed. A naive model predicting "On-Time" for all flights would score 77% accuracy while failing entirely to detect delays. We compensated using balanced class weighting (class_weight='balanced'), scale_pos_weight in XGBoost, and adjusted the decision threshold to 42% for risk sensitivity.

5 How does winter fog in Delhi affect flights departing Coimbatore or Chennai?

Answer: This is caused by reactionary delay (cascading turnaround delays). In India, an aircraft often operates multi-leg rotation schedules (e.g. DEL → BOM → CJB → MAA). If low visibility in Delhi delays the initial morning flight by 90 minutes, that incoming aircraft arrives late in Coimbatore, rippling delays across subsequent Tamil Nadu sectors throughout the day.

6 What makes the North-East Monsoon in Tamil Nadu unique in aviation analysis?

Answer: Unlike most of India which receives rainfall during the South-West Monsoon (June to September), Tamil Nadu (particularly Chennai and coastal districts) receives the bulk of its precipitation during the North-East Monsoon (October, November, December). Our dataset models this regional reality, where delay probability at MAA and CJB spikes in October-December due to convective cloud bursts and low cloud ceilings.
